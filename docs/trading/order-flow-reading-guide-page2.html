1Setup #2: the failed breakout (mean reversion)

Use this when the market is in balance. Price pokes out of value, can't hold, and snaps back to the middle.

1. Poke outPrice breaks above value area high (VAH).
2. CVD divergesPrice makes a higher high, CVD makes a lower high. Buyers are tired.
3. Back insideA bar closes back inside value. The breakout failed.
4. AggressionOn the retest of VAH, big sell bubbles print. Go short, stop just above them, target the POC.

2Footprint chart: looking inside a candle

Each row is one price. Left number = contracts sold at the bid (aggressive sellers). Right number = bought at the ask (aggressive buyers).

Imbalance rule (a common default): compare diagonally. If buys at a price are 3× or more the sells one row below, that's a buy imbalance (and the reverse for sells). Three or more in a row = "stacked", a strong footprint.

3Big trade bubbles: when they matter

5Sizing and managing the trade (worked numbers)

Example: $10,000 account, micro Nasdaq (MNQ). MNQ moves $2 per point; full-size NQ moves $20 per point.

Risk per trade (0.25% of $10,000)$25
Stop distance (entry to stop)3 points
Risk per MNQ contract (3 pts × $2)$6
Contracts (floor of $25 ÷ $6)4 MNQ ($24 risk)
Target 6 points away (2R), profit if hit+$48
Same trade on 1 full NQ (3 pts × $20)$60 risk: too big
Max loss for the day (3 losses, then stop)−$75

Educational illustration only, not financial advice. Practice on a simulated account first.
